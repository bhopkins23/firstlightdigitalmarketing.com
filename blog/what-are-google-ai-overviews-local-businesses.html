What Are Google AI Overviews and Why They Matter for Local Businesses

Google is answering your customers' questions before they ever click a link. Here is how AI Overviews work, what they mean for businesses in Bucks County, and what you can do about it.

By First Light Marketing | September 10, 2026 | 13 min read

Something big has changed about Google, and if you own a local business, it directly affects how customers find you. The next time you search for something on Google, pay attention to the top of the results page. Before the familiar list of blue links, before the map, you will likely see a large text box with an AI-generated summary that tries to answer your question on the spot. Google calls these AI Overviews, and they are reshaping how people discover and choose local businesses.

If you run a restaurant in Doylestown, a contracting business in Newtown, or a dental practice in Yardley, this change matters to you. The businesses that Google's AI decides to mention in these summaries get seen first. The ones it skips get pushed further down the page, out of sight for many potential customers. This article explains exactly what AI Overviews are, how they work, and what you can do to make sure your business is one of the ones Google recommends.

What Exactly Are Google AI Overviews?

Google AI Overviews are AI-generated answer panels that appear at the top of search results for a growing number of queries. When you type a question or search phrase into Google, the search engine now uses its AI model (called Gemini) to read through multiple web pages, extract the most useful information, and write a summary that answers your question directly in the search results.

Think of it like this: instead of Google handing you a stack of brochures and saying "figure it out," it now reads all those brochures for you and gives you a summary of what it found. Below the summary, it includes links to the specific websites it pulled information from — these are called citations, and they are the new premium real estate in search.

Here is what makes this different from a regular Google search. In the old model, Google ranked web pages and presented them as a list. You chose which one to click. In the new model, Google's AI chooses which sources to trust, pulls the relevant information from them, and presents a synthesized answer. You might not need to click anything at all — but if you do, you are far more likely to click one of the sources the AI cited.

Where AI Overviews Appear and How Often

AI Overviews do not appear for every search. Google activates them primarily for queries that involve research, comparison, or questions — the types of searches where a simple list of links is not the most helpful format. As of 2026, AI Overviews appear for roughly 30 to 40 percent of all Google searches, and that number continues to grow.

For local businesses, the most important thing to understand is that AI Overviews are especially common for the exact searches your potential customers are making. Searches like "best Italian restaurant near Doylestown," "reliable electrician in Bucks County," or "family dentist accepting new patients in Newtown" frequently trigger AI Overviews. These are the high-intent searches that drive real business, and they are exactly where AI Overviews are taking over.

How AI Overviews Work for Local Searches: Real Examples

To understand why this matters, let us walk through what actually happens when a potential customer in Bucks County searches for a local business.

Example: Finding a Restaurant in Doylestown

Imagine someone visiting Doylestown for the weekend types "best restaurants in Doylestown PA" into Google. Before AI Overviews, they would see a map pack with three restaurants and then a list of review sites, blog posts, and restaurant websites. They would click through several pages, read reviews, and eventually decide where to eat.

Now, with AI Overviews, Google reads those same review sites, blog posts, and restaurant websites, and generates a summary right at the top. That summary might say something like: "Doylestown has a thriving restaurant scene with several highly rated options. For Italian, [Restaurant A] is frequently recommended for its handmade pasta and BYOB policy. [Restaurant B] is known for farm-to-table American cuisine..." and so on. Below the summary, Google links to the sources it pulled from.

Here is the critical point: the restaurants named in that AI Overview get massive visibility. The ones not mentioned? Many potential diners will never even see them, because the AI Overview answered the question before they scrolled down.

Example: Hiring a Contractor in Newtown

A homeowner in Newtown searches "best kitchen remodeling contractors Bucks County." The AI Overview pulls information from contractor websites, review platforms, and local directories to generate a response that might read: "Several well-reviewed contractors serve the Bucks County area for kitchen remodeling. [Contractor A] specializes in custom cabinetry and has a 4.9-star rating across 80+ Google reviews. [Contractor B] offers full-service remodeling with transparent pricing..."

Notice what just happened. Google's AI did not just list websites — it made a recommendation. It evaluated businesses based on their reviews, website content, and online presence, and then told the searcher which contractors stand out and why. If your contracting business is not one of the ones named, you are invisible to that potential customer at the most critical moment in their decision-making process.

Example: Choosing a Dentist in Yardley

A family that recently moved to Yardley searches "family dentist near Yardley PA accepting new patients." The AI Overview might summarize: "Several family dental practices in the Yardley area are currently accepting new patients. [Dentist A] offers comprehensive family care including pediatric dentistry, with evening and weekend hours. [Dentist B] is known for a welcoming environment and has particularly strong reviews mentioning their gentle approach with children..."

For a dental practice, being cited in that overview could mean dozens of new patient inquiries from a single search. Not being cited means those potential patients went to a competitor whose online presence was stronger.

Key Takeaway

Google AI Overviews do not just list businesses — they recommend them. The AI evaluates your website content, reviews, and online presence to decide whether your business deserves to be mentioned. If it decides your competitor is a better source, your competitor gets the visibility and the customer.

How Google Decides Which Businesses to Cite

Understanding what goes into Google's AI decisions is essential if you want your business to appear in these overviews. Based on industry research and what we see working for our clients, several factors consistently determine which businesses get cited.

Strong Organic Search Rankings

The number one predictor of whether your site gets cited in an AI Overview is whether it already ranks well in traditional Google search results. Studies show that the vast majority of AI Overview citations come from pages that rank on page one for the relevant query. This means traditional SEO is not dead — it is actually the foundation that AI visibility is built on.

If your website does not rank on the first page of Google for your most important keywords, getting into AI Overviews is going to be very difficult. You need to get the fundamentals right first.

A Complete and Active Google Business Profile

For local queries, your Google Business Profile (GBP) is one of the most important data sources that AI Overviews draw from. A fully completed profile with accurate business information, service descriptions, hours, photos, and regular posts gives Google's AI much more material to work with when generating its summaries. An incomplete or outdated profile tells the AI that your business may not be a reliable source.

Your GBP is especially important for local SEO in the age of AI. It is not enough to simply claim your listing. You need to treat it as an active marketing channel — updating it regularly, adding new photos, posting updates, and responding to every review.

Customer Reviews: Quality and Quantity

Google's AI pays close attention to your review profile. Businesses with a high volume of genuine, detailed reviews are significantly more likely to be cited in AI Overviews than businesses with few or no reviews. The AI also reads the content of reviews — it picks up on specific themes, services, and attributes that reviewers mention frequently.

A dental practice with 120 reviews that consistently mention "gentle with kids" and "short wait times" gives the AI specific, useful information it can include in an overview. A practice with 8 generic five-star reviews does not give the AI much to work with.

Website Content That Answers Questions

AI systems are built to find and extract answers. When your website has content that directly answers the questions your potential customers are searching for, the AI has a clear reason to cite you. This means service pages with detailed descriptions, FAQ sections that address common questions, and blog content that covers topics in depth.

The content does not need to be written for search engines or stuffed with keywords. It needs to be genuinely useful, clearly structured, and comprehensive. Think about the questions a customer would ask you in a consultation and make sure those answers are on your website.

Structured Data Markup

Structured data — also called schema markup — is code added to your website that helps search engines understand what your content is about. For local businesses, LocalBusiness schema, FAQPage schema, and Review schema make it significantly easier for Google's AI to identify, categorize, and extract information from your pages. Most local business websites do not have this markup, which means adding it gives you an immediate advantage.

What Happens When You Do Not Optimize for AI Overviews

Some business owners hear about AI Overviews and think: "This does not affect me. My customers know who I am." Here is why that thinking is risky.

Your Competitors Get Cited Instead of You

AI Overviews are going to cite someone. If your business is not positioned to be that someone, your competitors will be. When a potential customer searches for the services you offer and the AI Overview mentions three businesses — none of which is yours — you have effectively been eliminated from the competition before the customer even knows you exist.

This is not hypothetical. It is happening right now across every industry and every local market, including Bucks County. The businesses that show up in AI Overviews are capturing attention and inquiries that would have been distributed more evenly in the old model of ten blue links.

Your Click-Through Rate Drops

Research shows that when an AI Overview appears on a search results page, click-through rates to organic results below it drop significantly. If the overview answers the searcher's question or recommends specific businesses, many users never scroll past it. Even if your website ranks on page one, being below an AI Overview that does not mention you means fewer people are clicking through to your site.

The Gap Gets Harder to Close Over Time

AI systems learn and reinforce their own patterns. If Google's AI consistently cites your competitor for a particular type of query, that pattern tends to persist and strengthen over time. The competitor accumulates more clicks, more engagement, and more review data — all of which further reinforces the AI's decision to cite them. Waiting to address this means the gap between you and your AI-visible competitors grows wider.

Key Takeaway

Not optimizing for AI Overviews does not mean maintaining the status quo. It means falling behind, because your competitors who do optimize will capture the visibility you are missing. The longer you wait, the wider the gap becomes.

What Your Business Should Do Now

The good news is that optimizing for AI Overviews is not an entirely new discipline. It builds on the same SEO fundamentals that have always driven results, with some specific additions. Here is a practical roadmap.

1. Audit Your Current AI Visibility

Start by searching for the queries that matter most to your business and observe what happens. Does an AI Overview appear? Is your business mentioned? Which competitors are being cited? Do this for at least 10 to 15 of your most important search terms to get a clear picture of where you stand. This gives you a baseline and helps you prioritize your efforts.

2. Strengthen Your SEO Foundation

Because AI Overviews primarily cite pages that already rank well organically, shoring up your traditional SEO is step one. This means ensuring your website has strong technical health, quality content on every service page, proper title tags and meta descriptions, fast loading times, and a mobile-friendly design. If you are not on page one for your core keywords, that is where to focus first.

3. Fully Optimize Your Google Business Profile

Fill out every section of your Google Business Profile completely. Add all of your services with descriptions. Upload high-quality photos regularly. Post updates at least once a week. Respond to every review — positive and negative — with thoughtful, specific replies. Make sure your business hours, phone number, and address are accurate and consistent with what appears on your website and other directories.

4. Add FAQ Sections to Your Key Pages

Every service page on your website should have a section of 5 to 8 frequently asked questions with clear, direct answers. These Q&A pairs are exactly the type of content that AI systems look for when generating overviews. Make sure the questions reflect what real customers actually ask you — not what you think sounds good for SEO. Then implement FAQPage schema markup so search engines can easily identify these Q&A pairs.

5. Create In-Depth Content That Demonstrates Expertise

A blog or resource section gives you the space to demonstrate deep expertise on topics related to your industry. A contractor can write about the remodeling process, common challenges with older Bucks County homes, and how to budget for a renovation. A dentist can write about pediatric dental care, cosmetic procedures, and preventive health. Each piece of quality content strengthens your topical authority and gives AI systems more material to cite.

This is where AI SEO strategy goes beyond traditional optimization. The content you create needs to be structured for extraction — clear headings, direct answers, specific data points — so that AI systems can easily pull useful information from it. To learn more about how AI is changing the broader search landscape, see our guide on what AI SEO is and how AI search is changing SEO.

6. Build Your Review Profile

Make it a priority to generate a steady stream of genuine customer reviews on Google. Ask satisfied customers to share their experience, and make the process easy for them. The goal is not just a high star rating — it is a volume of detailed reviews that mention specific services, qualities, and experiences. These details give AI systems concrete information to reference when generating overviews about businesses in your category.

7. Implement Structured Data Across Your Website

At minimum, your website should have LocalBusiness schema (with accurate name, address, phone, and service area), FAQPage schema on every page with FAQ content, Article schema on blog posts, and BreadcrumbList schema for site navigation. This structured data acts as a clear signal to AI systems about what your business does, where it operates, and what information each page contains.

Key Takeaway

Getting cited in AI Overviews requires a combination of strong traditional SEO, an active Google Business Profile, quality reviews, expert content, and structured data. No single tactic is enough on its own, but together they position your business as the kind of authoritative source that AI systems want to cite.

Frequently Asked Questions About Google AI Overviews

What are Google AI Overviews?

Google AI Overviews are AI-generated summary panels that appear at the top of Google search results for many queries. Instead of showing a simple list of links, Google uses its Gemini AI model to read multiple web pages, pull out the most relevant information, and present a synthesized answer with links to the sources it drew from. They appear for a growing number of searches, including many local business queries.

How do Google AI Overviews affect local businesses?

AI Overviews change local search by answering questions about local businesses directly in the search results. When someone searches for a service provider in their area, the AI Overview may name specific businesses, summarize their reviews, and compare their services — all before the user sees traditional search results. Businesses that get cited gain prominent visibility, while those that do not may lose traffic to competitors featured in the overview.

How can my business get cited in Google AI Overviews?

To get cited in AI Overviews, focus on building strong organic rankings through quality content and SEO, fully optimize your Google Business Profile with accurate information and regular updates, add FAQ sections with structured data markup to your service pages, earn genuine customer reviews, and create in-depth content that directly answers the questions your customers search for. Businesses that already rank well in traditional search are most likely to be cited.

Do AI Overviews replace regular Google search results?

No, AI Overviews do not replace traditional search results. They appear above the regular organic listings and map results for certain types of queries, particularly informational and research-oriented searches. The traditional results still appear below. However, because AI Overviews sit at the very top of the page, many users get their answer there and never scroll down, which can reduce clicks to websites that are not cited in the overview.

What types of searches trigger AI Overviews for local businesses?

AI Overviews most commonly appear for local searches that involve comparison or research, such as "best dentist in Doylestown," "how to choose a contractor in Bucks County," or "affordable landscaping services near me." They also appear for questions about local services like "what does a kitchen remodel cost in PA" or "how often should I service my HVAC." Simple navigational searches for a specific business name are less likely to trigger an AI Overview.

Is it too late for my business to optimize for AI Overviews?

Not at all. AI Overviews are still relatively new, and most local businesses have not yet taken specific steps to optimize for them. This means there is a real window of opportunity for businesses that act now. The strategies that help you get cited in AI Overviews — strong website content, an optimized Google Business Profile, customer reviews, and structured data — also improve your traditional SEO, so the effort benefits your overall online visibility regardless.
